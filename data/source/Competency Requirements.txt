Competency Requirements
Competencies include the following four requirements:

Written Communication (BC, IC)
Oral Communication (OC)
Quantitative Experience (QE)
Success in college and the ability to function as an educated citizen require a series of fundamental skills that underlie and make possible the acquisition of knowledge. Competencies may be demonstrated by satisfactory completion of designated courses or by earning appropriate scores on designated examinations.

Competencies or skills are preconditions for higher education, and thus Competency requirements should be completed early in a student's baccalaureate degree program. Students who fail to meet the specified deadline will be allowed two additional semesters (or equivalent) in which to satisfy the Competency requirement. After the two-semester limit, students who have not satisfied the requirement may be barred from enrolling in courses other than those which satisfy the Competency requirement until the requirement has been completed.

The following general principles apply to all Competency requirements:

Students who satisfy any Competency requirement by passing a prescribed Wayne State University placement, qualifying, screening, competency, or proficiency examination shall be excused from equivalent course work but will receive NO course credit.
Course credit granted for satisfactory completion of an Advanced Placement, CLEP, International Baccalaureate, or Departmental examination will satisfy the appropriate Competency or Inquiry requirement; credit so earned will be applicable to a baccalaureate degree.
Courses used to satisfy Competency requirements shall not generally also be used to satisfy Inquiry requirements.
Competency requirements BC, IC, and QE must be completed with a grade of C or higher.
Written Communication (BC, IC)
Writing ability is a cornerstone of academic study and is often considered the touchstone of a university education. Skill and effectiveness in writing serve an individual throughout their life — in career, in community, and in social and leisure activities. An ability to write well allows an individual to effectively address general audiences as well as specialized audiences within professional fields. Moreover, writing is a skill that that requires constant renewal. The Written Communication requirements are structured not only to provide training in how to write well, but also to insure that writing skills continue to be exercised and enhanced throughout the undergraduate years. The progression of the Written Communication requirements reflect the important notion of 'writing across the curriculum,' and Intermediate Composition courses therefore build from the skills learned in Basic Composition courses.

Basic Composition (BC) Requirement
All students must demonstrate competence in basic English composition prior to completing thirty credits. Basic composition competence shall be determined by satisfactory completion of a designated course that includes instruction in multiple genres of writing, or its course equivalent or earning credit for Basic Composition through a national standardized test. 

BC has four program learning outcomes.

After successful completion of this requirement, students will be able to demonstrate their ability to:

Describe the arguments, purpose, context, and audience in college-level texts.
Reference credible, relevant sources in ethical ways.
Employ the conventions of a genre.
Explain their own writing choices and process.
Satisfactory completion requires a grade of C or better.  Course(s) satisfying the Basic Composition requirement include:

Students demonstrating completion of a transferred IC/ICN course with no BC course on their record will be eligible to have BC waived.

Intermediate Composition (IC) Requirement
All students must complete satisfactorily a designated intermediate, or more advanced, course in which the teaching of English composition and rhetoric is a major component prior to completing seventy-five credits. 

IC has four program learning outcomes.

After successful completion of this requirement, students will be able to demonstrate their ability to:

Utilize rhetorical practices appropriate to academic or public audiences.
Reference credible, relevant sources in ethical ways.
Synthesize academic research that is relevant and appropriate for a specific audience.
Construct a research argument that responds to scholarly questions of a specific audience.
Satisfactory completion requires a grade of C or better. Courses currently approved for Intermediate Composition are:

Oral Communication (OC) Requirement 
Educated persons should be comfortable in situations which require them to make oral presentations, be able to convince others of a point of view, or make appropriate remarks in an informal setting. Along with an ability to write cogently, communicating orally is mentioned most frequently by employers and others who evaluate the preparedness of college students as a fundamental skill to be able to compete in contemporary society. Consequently, oral communication is a crucial skill needed for success in virtually every field of endeavor.

OC has five program learning outcomes.

After successful completion of this requirement, students will be able to demonstrate their ability to:

Adapt to the needs of a distinct speaking situation.
Reference credible, relevant sources in ethical ways.
Utilize relevant presentation technology in an oral presentation.
Employ verbal techniques for effective delivery in an oral presentation.
Employ non-verbal techniques for effective delivery in an oral presentation.
Courses satisfying the Oral Communication requirement include:

Transferring credit received for successful completion of a comparable course taken at another college or university.
Quantitative Experience (QE) Requirement
Individuals with competency in quantitative skills possess the ability to reason and solve problems from a wide range of authentic contexts and everyday life situations. They can interpret quantitative data and use that analysis to provide support for sophisticated arguments. They can clearly communicate those arguments in a variety of formats as appropriate. This requirement is aimed at developing such skills in all of our students.

QE has four program learning outcomes.

After successful completion of the QE requirement, students will be able to:

Convert real-world information into appropriate mathematical form.
Perform mathematical or symbolic computations relevant to a multistep problem.
Draw reasonable conclusions based on quantitative evidence.
Communicate arguments or interpretations supported by quantitative evidence.
