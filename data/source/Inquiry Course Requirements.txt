Inquiry Course Requirements
Inquiry groups include:

Civic Literacy Inquiry (CIV)
Cultural Inquiry (CI)
Global Learning Inquiry (GL)
Natural Scientific Inquiry (NSI)
Social Inquiry (SI)
US Perspectives (USP)
Inquiry courses introduce students to differing perspectives, methodologies, and questions that shape the production of knowledge. In these courses students will learn how scholars think in different disciplines, and they will also engage in inquiry themselves, using diverse disciplinary methodologies to ask questions, analyze data, and make their own evidence-based arguments.

Through courses that fulfill Inquiry requirements, students will:

Gain exposure to different disciplinary ways of understanding the world;
Develop basic competencies in these disciplinary methodologies; and
Apply disciplinary methodologies to analyze relevant data or examples.
The three primary categories of inquiry include Social Inquiry, Cultural Inquiry, and Natural Scientific Inquiry. There are three additional cross-inquiry categories: Civic Literacy; Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion; and Global Learning. Cross-inquiry courses provide focus on themes that may cut across the primary inquiry categories, such that any cross-inquiry courses could be conceivably be found in a primary inquiry category. For example, a course focused on Global Learning outcomes could be offered from the perspective of the social sciences and/or the arts and humanities.

Each student must complete a course of at least three credits from each of the six inquiry groups; the Natural Scientific Inquiry (NSI) group requires a second NSI course with a lab section, for a minimum total of seven courses.

NOTE: Schools and Colleges may also have specific requirements, such that careful course selection can lead to meeting both General Education and College requirements. Please consult the College/School listing for specific requirements.

Civic Literacy Inquiry (CIV)
CIV has four program learning outcomes.

After successful completion of the CIV requirement, each student will be able to:

Describe concepts and contexts relevant to US history, political institutions, and democratic principles.
Describe the significance of citizenship status and national identity, past or present.
Describe forms, barriers, and facilitators to civic participation or engagement by individuals and groups.
Describe the economic, historic, political, and social bases for conflict and cooperation between groups.

Cultural Inquiry (CI)
CI has three program learning outcomes.

Analyze an artistic practice, cultural form, artifact, or philosophical idea.
Compare artistic practices, cultural forms, artifacts, or philosophical ideas.
Relate the significance of artistic practices, cultural forms, artifacts, or philosophical ideas to historical or contemporary societal subjects.

Natural Scientific Inquiry (NSI)
NSI has four program learning outcomes.

After successful completion of the NSI requirement, students will be able to demonstrate their ability to:

Explain scientific concepts related to natural phenomena.
Discriminate between valid and invalid inferences made using scientific concepts and evidence.
Describe the relevance of scientific concepts to historical or contemporary societal subjects.
Describe data collected via the scientific method (required for lab courses only, optional for non-lab courses).

US Perspectives (USP)
US Perspectives has four program learning outcomes, which focus on the U.S as their primary context:

After successful completion of the USP requirement, each student will be able to:

Explain how groups, perspectives, or traditions across the United States have shaped and been shaped by U.S. history, institutions, practices, or policies.  
Explain how cultural values, beliefs, and social identities influence individual or group behavior.  
Explain competing values or perspectives that arise in complex domestic (U.S.) situations involving multiple communities, traditions, or viewpoints.  
Analyze approaches taken by individuals, institutions, or communities to address questions of pluralism, intergroup relations, or civic life at the local or national level.
